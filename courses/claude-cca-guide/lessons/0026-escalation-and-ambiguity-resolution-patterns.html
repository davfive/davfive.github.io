CCA-F · Domain 5 · Task Statement 5.2

Escalation & Ambiguity Resolution Patterns

Escalate on the right triggers — not on how complicated or emotionally charged the case merely feels.

Real triggers vs. unreliable proxies

Legitimate escalation triggers: an explicit customer request for a human, a genuine policy exception or gap (not just a complex-seeming case), or a real inability to make further progress. Notably unreliable as escalation signals: sentiment analysis (frustration doesn't necessarily mean the case needs a human) and the model's own self-reported confidence score — neither reliably tracks actual case complexity.

Two ways to get this wrong in opposite directions Escalating immediately just because a customer sounds upset, when the issue is actually within the agent's capability to resolve — versus not escalating when a customer has explicitly asked for a human, and continuing to investigate instead of honoring that request right away.

Handling ambiguity without guessing

When policy is silent or ambiguous on a specific request (e.g., a competitor price-match request when policy only covers own-site price adjustments), escalate rather than improvise an answer. When a tool lookup returns multiple matching customer records, ask for an additional identifying detail rather than heuristically picking one — a wrong guess here has real consequences.

Check your understanding

Scenario
A customer explicitly says, "I want to speak to a human about this," but the issue described sounds straightforward and likely resolvable by the agent. What's the most appropriate response?
AHonor the explicit request and escalate to a human immediately, without first attempting to resolve it.
BAcknowledge the request but attempt to resolve the issue first, escalating only if it turns out to be complex.
CIgnore the request since the underlying issue is within the agent's capability to resolve on its own.
DAsk the customer to confirm they really want a human before proceeding either way.
Correct The guide draws this distinction directly: an explicit request for a human should be honored immediately, distinct from the case where the agent offers to resolve first because the customer hasn't asked to escalate.
Not quite Attempting resolution first is the correct approach only when the customer hasn't explicitly asked for a human — here they have. Ignoring an explicit request outright disregards stated customer preference entirely. Asking them to confirm adds unnecessary friction to a preference they've already stated clearly.

CCA-F · Reference · Domain 5 (15% of exam)

Context Management & Reliability

Print-friendly compression of all 6 Task Statements in this domain.

5.1 — Preserving context over long interactions

  • Progressive summarization blurs numbers, dates, and stated expectations unless pulled into a persistent "case facts" block.
  • "Lost in the middle": models attend reliably to start/end, not necessarily the middle of long inputs.

5.2 — Escalation & ambiguity resolution

  • Real triggers: explicit human request, genuine policy gap, real inability to progress. Not reliable: sentiment, self-reported confidence.
  • Multiple record matches → ask for another identifier, never guess heuristically.

5.3 — Error propagation across multi-agent systems

  • Report failure type + what was attempted + partial results + alternatives — not a generic status.
  • Access failure (timeout) ≠ valid empty result. Don't suppress errors silently or kill the whole workflow on one failure.

5.4 — Context in large codebase exploration

  • Long sessions degrade to generic "typical pattern" answers — externalize memory via scratchpad files.
  • Crash recovery: agents export state to a manifest; coordinator reloads and injects it on resume.

5.5 — Human review & confidence calibration

  • Aggregate accuracy (e.g. 97%) can mask a specific document type or field performing much worse.
  • Stratified sampling of high-confidence extractions + field-level calibrated confidence scores route review attention correctly.

5.6 — Provenance & uncertainty in synthesis

  • Preserve structured claim-source mappings through every synthesis stage.
  • Conflicting sources: annotate both values with attribution — never silently pick one or average them.
  • Always carry publication/collection dates so temporal differences aren't mistaken for contradictions.
Cross-cutting ruleStructure beats compression whenever a downstream reader (human or coordinator) needs to make a real decision — collapse only what's genuinely safe to lose.

Glossary (Domain 5)

Lost in the middle: models attending reliably to the start/end of long inputs while dropping mid-document findings.
Case facts block: persistent, non-summarized record of transactional facts carried in every prompt.
Coverage annotation: synthesis metadata distinguishing well-supported findings from gaps.
